The model is a small block of a unidirectional composite: parallel fibers (gray) in a matrix (amber). The block stands for the whole material, so we call it a representative volume element (RVE).

Learn

1. The representative volume element

A composite has fibers a few micrometers wide. To find the stiffness of the whole material, we study a small block that holds enough fibers to represent it: the RVE. Here the fibers are in a hexagonal pattern, with axis 1 along the fibers.

The composite is the same in all directions across the fibers (\(E_2 = E_3\)): it is transversely isotropic. Five constants describe it: \(E_1\), \(E_2\), \(\nu_{12}\), \(G_{12}\), and \(G_{23}\) (or \(\nu_{23}\)). Carbon and aramid fibers are transversely isotropic too; glass and boron fibers are isotropic.

2. Fiber volume fraction and density

\(V_f\) is the part of the volume that is fiber; the matrix has \(V_m = 1 - V_f\). In a hexagonal pattern with fibers of radius \(r\) at a distance \(a\):

\[ V_f = \frac{2\pi r^2}{\sqrt{3}\,a^2} \]

When the fibers touch (\(a = 2r\)), \(V_f = \pi / (2\sqrt{3}) \approx 0.907\). That is why the slider stops at 0.90. The density follows a simple mix:

\[ \rho_c = \rho_f V_f + \rho_m V_m \]

3. The rule of mixtures (ROM)

Along the fibers, fiber and matrix stretch together, like parallel springs. The major Poisson's ratio follows the same mix:

\[ E_1 = E_{f1} V_f + E_m V_m \]

\[ \nu_{12} = \nu_{f12} V_f + \nu_m V_m \]

In shear, they act like springs in a row:

\[ \frac{1}{G_{12}} = \frac{V_f}{G_{f12}} + \frac{V_m}{G_m} \]

The same row rule for \(E_2\) gives too low values. This lesson uses an improved form, with \(s = \sqrt{V_f}\):

\[ \frac{1}{E_2} = \frac{s}{E_{f2}\,s + E_m (1 - s)} + \frac{1 - s}{E_m} \]

The rule of mixtures gives no \(G_{23}\) and no \(\nu_{23}\).

4. Chamis

Chamis uses the square root of \(V_f\) for the properties across the fibers:

\[ E_2 = \frac{E_m}{1 - \sqrt{V_f}\,(1 - E_m / E_{f2})} \]

\[ G_{12} = \frac{G_m}{1 - \sqrt{V_f}\,(1 - G_m / G_{f12})} \]

\(G_{23}\) has the same form with \(G_{f23}\), and \(\nu_{23} = E_2 / (2 G_{23}) - 1\).

5. Composite cylinders (CCA)

Each fiber sits in a ring of matrix, and the rings fill the space. The theory of elasticity then gives exact values of \(E_1\), \(\nu_{12}\), \(G_{12}\), and the plane-strain bulk modulus \(K_{23}\); a three-phase model gives \(G_{23}\). For example:

\[ G_{12} = G_m \,\frac{G_{f12}(1 + V_f) + G_m V_m} {G_{f12} V_m + G_m (1 + V_f)} \]

Then \(E_2\) follows from \(K_{23}\) and \(G_{23}\). The fiber enters with its own transversely isotropic \(K_{23}\), so CCA gives back the fiber at \(V_f = 1\).

6. Mori-Tanaka (MT)

Each fiber feels the average strain of the matrix around it. For a unidirectional ply this gives closed formulas. MT and CCA give the same \(E_1\), \(\nu_{12}\), \(G_{12}\), and \(K_{23}\); they differ a little in \(G_{23}\), and so in \(E_2\).

7. Along the fibers, the models agree

All four models give almost the same \(E_1\): along the fibers, the stiff fibers carry the load. Across the fibers, the load must pass through the soft matrix, so the result depends on how the model describes that path. The models differ there by 10 to 20 %.

A stiffer matrix helps much more across the fibers than along them.

Look at carbon fibers in aluminum in the chart E1 and E2 against Vf: \(E_2\) falls when \(V_f\) grows. A carbon fiber is stiff along its axis (230 GPa) but soft across it (\(E_{f2} = 22\) GPa), softer than aluminum (71 GPa). Across the fibers, more fiber then means a softer composite.

8. Stiffness for its weight

Composites win on specific stiffness \(E/\rho\): the stiffness for the same weight. Carbon/epoxy at \(V_f = 0.6\) has \(E_1/\rho\) about 3 times that of steel or aluminum. Across the fibers it is much lower than the metals: a unidirectional ply is good in one direction only. That is why real parts stack plies at several angles (see the lesson Is it symmetric?).

The rule of mixtures, as in the app:

def rule_of_mixtures(fiber: Fiber, em: float, num: float,
                     vf: float) -> tuple[float, ...]:
    """Return the properties by the rule of mixtures."""
    vm = 1.0 - vf
    gm = shear_modulus(em, num)
    root = np.sqrt(vf)
    e1 = vf * fiber.e1 + vm * em
    e2 = 1.0 / (root / (fiber.e2 * root + em * (1.0 - root)) + (1.0 - root) / em)
    g12 = fiber.g12 * gm / (gm * vf + fiber.g12 * vm)
    nu12 = vf * fiber.nu12 + vm * num
    return e1, e2, g12, np.nan, nu12, np.nan
